# Licensing provenance audit

This record supports the repository transition from MIT to `LGPL-3.0-or-later`.

## Repository contributor inventory

GitHub's repository contributor API was reviewed before the transition. The dominant author is `shuntatsu`. Other recorded identities are automation or AI-assisted development identities used in the project workflow, including `github-actions[bot]`, Anthropic's `Claude <noreply@anthropic.com>`, architecture-hardening automation, Crave AI Agent, trade-rl automation identities, and OpenAI-labelled automation commits.

A sample of the `claude` commit history was inspected and showed commits authored and committed as `Claude <noreply@anthropic.com>` for AI-assisted project work rather than a separately identified human contributor submitting independent copyright material.

No distinct external human contributor account was identified in the repository contributor inventory used for this transition.

## Transition decision

The repository owner is treating the automation and AI-assisted commits as work produced under the owner's project direction and is relicensing the maintained repository source under `LGPL-3.0-or-later`.

Historical revisions that were already distributed under MIT remain available under their historical MIT grant. `LICENSES/MIT.txt` preserves that text; the transition does not attempt to revoke historical permissions.

## Third-party boundaries

Third-party dependencies, generated dependency metadata, upstream license texts, trademarks, and copied upstream material remain governed by their respective rights. New third-party code must not be copied into the repository merely because its dependency can be imported.

The planned NautilusTrader integration is dependency-based. NautilusTrader remains an upstream Nautech Systems project licensed under GNU LGPL v3; Trade RL does not claim ownership of NautilusTrader code or trademarks.

## Audit limitations

This is an engineering provenance record, not a legal opinion. GitHub author strings and contributor counts are provenance signals rather than proof of copyright ownership. Any future evidence of a material external human contribution with incompatible or uncertain relicensing rights must be investigated before redistribution of the affected material.
